# CorePath Australian health and wellbeing partner standard

Status: the founder approved the five baseline requirements and Australia as the first trial. Health and wellbeing is the selected first field. The four health-specific requirements below are proposed for founder review; no real business has been qualified or emailed by this implementation.

## Purpose and scope

A partner must provide a genuine benefit within its competence, respect the customer's autonomy, and meet every applicable requirement. Fees and CorePath's proposed 10% commission never influence qualification or ranking. This assessment is an internal partnership decision, not a general consumer star rating, a clinical recommendation or a guarantee of outcomes.

Apply the standard to the actual service, practitioner, location and jurisdiction. A fitness instructor, a dietitian, a counsellor, a massage therapist, a health-food shop and a medical clinic need different credentials and risk checks. Legal registration alone does not establish quality; absence from AHPRA does not automatically disqualify an unregulated occupation. Document whether a licence or registration is required for the specific activity.

## Five approved baseline requirements

| Requirement | What must be established | Example evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity and licensing | The operating entity, contact details, location, responsible people and required licences are verifiable and current. Relevant restrictions are understood. | Current official business/professional registers, licensing authority records; applicable insurance confirmation. |
| Clear pricing and terms | Customers can understand the full price, recurring fees, cancellation, refunds and material conditions before committing. No hidden charges or pressure selling. | Current published terms and price information; a checked service quote where public prices are impractical. |
| Credible quality evidence | The business can show that it competently provides the service it advertises. Evidence is relevant to that service and is proportionate to risk. | Appropriate qualifications, independent accreditation/audit, relevant service outcomes and substantiated quality records. Reviews may contribute; review volume, popularity and polished marketing are insufficient. |
| Working complaints process | There is an accessible contact route, a clear complaints procedure, a fair correction/refund process and an appropriate escalation path. | Published process, accountable contact and checked handling arrangements. Do not request identifiable patient complaints. |
| Safety and regulatory standing | Material risks and applicable duties have been checked. There is no unresolved, substantiated serious concern incompatible with safe service. | Relevant regulator/register checks, published restrictions and documented safeguards. Lack of search results is not proof that no concerns exist. |

## Four proposed health requirements

| Requirement | What must be established |
| --- | --- |
| Credentials and scope | Verify registrations where required, current conditions/restrictions, appropriate qualifications and competence for the advertised service. Unregulated roles are described honestly. Staff do not misrepresent protected titles or provide services beyond their scope. Check appropriate insurance where applicable. |
| Honest claims and evidence | Claims accurately reflect their evidence level, with uncertainty and limitations made clear. Limited research is not itself a failing grade for the provider. No guaranteed cures, fabricated testimonials or misleading diagnostic/treatment claims. A transparent, competent provider of a suitably safe wellbeing service may qualify even when research into that approach is limited. Claims to diagnose, treat or cure disease require stronger supporting evidence than general wellbeing claims. Assess natural, traditional and conventional approaches on evidence and risk; none receives automatic endorsement. Products and services must meet their applicable advertising rules. |
| Informed consent and referral | Explain relevant risks, limitations, costs and alternatives in understandable language. Have proportionate screening, safeguarding and referral/escalation arrangements. Recognise when a person's needs exceed the provider's competence; do not discourage appropriate care or suggest changing prescribed treatment outside professional scope. |
| Health privacy and conflicts | Explain collection, use, storage, access, correction and sharing of sensitive information. Obtain the necessary consent, protect confidentiality and avoid unnecessary collection. Disclose relevant financial interests and referral relationships. CorePath's commercial terms must not override suitability or independent recommendations. |

## Natural, traditional and emerging approaches

The founder's direction is explicit: do not rule an approach out solely because it is natural or under-researched. Investigate each practice, product and claim independently. Consider traditional knowledge, relevant practitioner experience, observational work and controlled research, while explaining what each can and cannot establish. Do not silently treat traditional use or testimony as proof of a clinical effect.

Distinguish well-supported evidence, promising but limited evidence, documented traditional use, unresolved uncertainty and evidence of ineffectiveness or harm. Absence of adequate research is not proof of ineffectiveness, effectiveness or safety. Business qualification assesses honest conduct, competence, proportionate safety and suitability; it is not a blanket endorsement of every offered treatment. A reviewer can mark the claims requirement as met when the provider honestly represents limited evidence and stays within appropriate bounds. Missing safety information still needs review, and stronger clinical claims need stronger evidence.

## Future independent research

The founder intends CorePath to fund research into promising, under-researched approaches as resources permit. This is a funding-dependent ambition, not an operating programme or promised outcome. The feedback bot may identify questions and recurring concerns; feedback and popularity are not clinical proof. Priorities should reflect potential benefit, evidence gaps, feasibility and risk rather than partner commissions. Future studies need appropriate independent expertise, ethical review where applicable, clear methods, safety oversight, declared conflicts and reporting of positive, negative and inconclusive findings.

## Decision rules

- **Qualified:** every applicable baseline and approved sector requirement is supported by reviewed, current evidence. The country/sector policy must be approved before outreach.
- **Needs review:** evidence is missing, stale, conflicting, disputed or unclear. Unknown is not a failing grade. A new or small business can supply proportionate evidence; it is not penalised for low review volume.
- **Not qualified:** current, reviewed evidence establishes that a mandatory requirement is not met. Record the exact reason and evidence. Reassessment is available after a correction or improvement.
- Do not average away a failed safety requirement. Where a numerical score is shown, it means the percentage of this checklist met and appears only when all requirements have determinate findings. It is not a clinical quality score.
- Keep these assessments private during the trial. Give a business a route to correct information and challenge a decision. Distinguish an allegation, an open investigation, a substantiated finding, a resolved issue and an ongoing restriction.
- Proposed operational default: recheck critical evidence within 90 days, sooner if a source expires or new information appears. An invitation rechecks eligibility immediately before dispatch. Evidence withdrawal or policy withdrawal blocks queued invitations.

## Separate commercial eligibility

Check the specific profession's rules on referral payments, conflicts and inducements before offering the proposed 10% model. Record the review, its source and the basis for compatibility. A business may pass the quality standard while being ineligible for that commercial arrangement. Do not lower its quality rating, create an alternative arrangement without a decision, or claim the referral model is lawful for every health profession. No invitation is queued until this separate check is documented.

## Separate permission to email

Qualification does not establish permission to send a commercial message. Record current express consent or a specifically reviewed basis for inferred consent for the actual business address and message. Merely finding an email address online is insufficient. Do not use harvested address lists. Identify CorePath and provide working contact details and a free, simple opt-out. The system suppresses opt-outs immediately; do not evade one by switching business records or sender addresses.
